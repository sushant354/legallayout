       The Court :- The present application has been taken out by the decree-
holder  seeking  transmission  of  the  decree  dated  12th  March,  2014,  passed  in
CS  No.281  of  2011,  for  execution  before  the  competent  Court  at  Thane,
Maharashtra, as per the prayers in the Notice of Motion.
2
       By the said decree, the suit was decreed in favour of the decree-holder
for  a  sum  of  Rs.48,00,000/-  together  with  post-decretal  simple  interest  @7%
per annum from the date of the decree till realization.
       The  appeal  preferred  against  the  decree  was  dismissed  by  the  Division
Bench on 22nd July, 2014, which had not been assailed thereafter in the higher
forum to the knowledge of the petitioner. Therefore, there is nothing on record
to indicate that the decree has thereafter been interfered with.  The decree has,
thus, attained finality, while the decretal dues remain unsatisfied.
       The  decree-holder  had  earlier  initiated  execution  proceedings  being
EC/495/2015.    By  an  order  dated  13th  July,  2018,  the  decree-holder  was
required  to  furnish  the  whereabouts  of  the  judgment-debtors.  At  that  stage,
despite  efforts,  the  decree-holder  was  unable  to  obtain  particulars  of  any
movable or immovable property of the judgment-debtors within the jurisdiction
of the Court.
       The  position  subsequently  altered.    Upon  further  inquiry,  the  decree-
holder learnt existence of an immovable property, viz. Flat No.1101 on the 11th
floor of building no.B-05, Sea Breeze Co-operative Housing Society, Plot No.-16,
Sector-16, Nerul, Navi Mumbai, Maharashtra-400 703 stated belonging  to the
judgment-debtors,  falling  within  the  jurisdiction  of  the  competent  Court  at
Thane.
       Pursuant to the order dated 3rd February, 2026, the Registry took steps
for service of notice upon the judgment-debtors.  The report of the Senior Police
Officer,  Nerul  Police  Station,  Navi  Mumbai,  placed  before  the  Court,  recorded
3
that the judgment-debtors could not be found at the aforesaid address.  Their
son  was  met  namely,  Mr.  Gaurav  Krishna  Kant  Laddha  at  the  aforesaid
address  who  admitted  the  judgment-debtors  were  his  parents  and  owing  to  a
family  dispute,  had  ceased  to  reside  with  him  and  were  presently  residing  at
Akola  (Vidarbha,  Maharashtra).  The  report  consequently  records  inability  to
serve notice for lack of relevant information about the defendants.
       The  police  report  does  not  indicate  the  present  residence  of  the
judgment-debtors  at  Nerul.   Its  significance  lies  in  demonstrating  the  steps  to
have  been  taken  pursuant  to  the  direction  of  this  Court  and  the  practical
difficulty  encountered  in  locating  the  judgment-debtors.    The  material
circumstance  for  the  present  application  remains  the  disclosure  of  the
immovable property within the territorial jurisdiction of the Court at Thane and
the  absence  of  sufficient  executable  property  within  the  jurisdiction  of  this
Court.
       The  Learned  Advocate  representing  the  applicant  referred  to  the
provisions  of  Sections  38,  39(1)(b)  and  Section  39(1)(d)  of  the  Civil  Procedure
Code urging that this Court under the facts and circumstances of the case, in
absence  of  any  executable  property  within  the  jurisdiction  of  this  Court  can
grant the prayer as stated in the Notice of Motion.
       Section 38 of the Civil Procedure Code, 1908, contemplates execution of
a decree either by the Court which passed it or by the Court to which it is sent
for execution.
4
       Section  39  of  the  Civil  Procedure  Code,  1908,  provides  the  machinery
for  such  transmission.  In  particular,  Section  39(1)(b)  of  the  Civil  Procedure
Code  permits  transfer  where  the  judgment-debtor  has  insufficient  property
within  the  jurisdiction  of  the  Court  which  passed  the  decree  but  has  property
within the jurisdiction of another competent Court. Section 39(1)(d) of the Civil
Procedure  Code  further  permits  transfer  where,  for  any  other  reason  to  be
recorded in writing, the Court considers that the decree should be executed by
another  Court.  Section  39(3)  of  Civil  Procedure  Code  prescribes  the  test  for
competence of the transferee Court.
       The  present  case  falls  within  the  statutory  contemplation  of  the
aforesaid  provisions.    The  decree  has  attained  finality.  The  decretal  amount
remains unpaid. The earlier execution could not be effectively pursued for want
of  particulars  of  executable  assets.  A  specific  immovable  property  of  the
judgment-debtor has subsequently been disclosed and/or traced out within the
jurisdiction of the competent Court at Thane.
       The  fact  that  the  property  is  situated  in  another  State  attracts  the
provision  of  Section  40  of  the  Civil  Procedure  Code  which  provides  for
transmission  of  a  decree  for  execution  to  a  Court  in  another  State.  The  Court
which  receives  the  decree  thereafter  exercises  the  statutory  powers
contemplated by the provisions of Section 42 of the Civil Procedure Code.  The
transferee Court executes the decree with the same powers as if the decree has
been passed by itself.
       The  procedural  requirements  for  transmission  are  provided  in  Order
XXI  Rule  6  of  the  Civil  Procedure  Code  requiring  the  transmitting  Court  to
5
forward  the  requisite  certified  copy  of  the  decree,  certificate  regarding
satisfaction  of  the  decree  and  other  necessary  papers.    The  transmission,
therefore,  does  not  involve  any  fresh  adjudication  of  the  rights  determined  by
the decree.  It merely places the final adjudication before the Court possessing
the territorial competence to enforce it.
       The decree-holder cannot reasonably require to continue before a Court
within  whose  jurisdiction  no  sufficient  executable  asset  has  been  identified
when  the  property  of  the  judgment-debtor/s  has  subsequently  brought  to  its
notice  within  the  jurisdiction  of  another  competent  Court.    The  statutory
scheme  of  Section  38  and  Section  42  of  the  Civil  Procedure  Code  exists
precisely to prevent  the territorial limits of the Court which  passed the decree
from  frustrating  its  execution.  Section  39  of  the  Civil  Procedure  Code  itself
recognizes such circumstances and permits transmission on recorded reasons.
       In  the  circumstances  disclosed,  this  Court  is  satisfied  that  the
requirements for transmission of the decree are met and that the prayer of the
decree-holder merits acceptance.  Accordingly, prayers (a) and (b) of the Notice
of Motion are allowed.
       The  decree  dated  12th  March,  2014,  passed  in  CS  No.280  of  2011  be
transmitted  for  execution  to  the  competent  Court  at  Thane,  Maharashtra  in
accordance  with  the  provisions  of  Sections  39  and  40  of  the  Code  of  Civil
Procedure, 1908.
       The Registrar, Original Side, shall prepare and forward to the competent
executing Court at Thane:-
6
       i.  Certified copy of the decree dated 12th March, 2014;
       ii.  Certificate  contemplated  under  Order  XXI  Rule  6  regarding  the
             extent to which the decree remains satisfied;
       iii.  The requisite papers relating to the earlier execution proceedings;
       iv.  The schedule of costs and
       v.  Such  other  documents  as  are  required  for  transmission  and
             execution of the decree in accordance with law.
       The  competent  executing  Court  shall  proceed  with  in  accordance  with
law  and  shall  exercise  the  powers  available  to  it  under  Section  42  of  the  Civil
Procedure Code.
       The  disclosure  of  property  at  Nerul  shall  not  be  construed  as  an
adjudication upon its title or upon the rights of any third party. Any question
arising  in  execution  shall  be  dealt  with  by  the  competent  executing  Court  in
accordance with law. The result of execution shall be certified to this Court in
terms of Section 41 of the Civil Procedure Code.
       The prayer ( c ) is allowed to the aforesaid extent.
       The instant application being GA/5/2025 is, accordingly, disposed of.
       Urgent certified photocopy of this order, if applied for, be supplied to the
parties upon compliance with all requisite formalities.
