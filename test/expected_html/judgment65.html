1. Heard Mr. Birendra Kumar, learned counsel for the appellant and Mr. Amit Kumar Das, learned senior counsel for the NIA.

2. This appeal is directed against the order dated 30-05-2026 passed by Sri Abhimanyu Kumar, learned Additional Judicial Commissioner-XVI-cum-Exclusive Special Judge, NIA, Ranchi, in connection with Misc.

Criminal Application No. 1027/2026 corresponding to Special (NIA) Case No. 03/2022 arising out of RC-02/2022/NIA/RNC, whereby and whereunder, the prayer for bail of the appellant has been rejected.

3. The prosecution case in brief is that an information was received by the Police that Ravindra Ganjhu, Regional Commander of CPI (Maoist), a proscribed terrorist organization along with active cadres of CPI (Maoist) Balram Oraon, Muneshwar Ganjhu, Balak Ganjhu, Dinesh Nagesia, Aghanu Ganjhu, Lajim Ansari, Markush Nagesia, Sanjay Nagesia, Sheela Kherwar, Lalita Devi and 45-50 other cadres had assembled in the forest area of Bulbul and were planning to execute an incident against the security forces and in the bauxite mines area and on such information, a team was constituted and extensive search operation was carried out. It has been alleged that on 17-02-2022, while the search team was proceeding towards Bahabar Jungle, it was ambushed and fired upon indiscriminately by the extremists. On retaliation, the extremists had fled away. A search was conducted and several arms and ammunitions were recovered. The search continued on the next day, when the search team found 7-8 pitthu (backpack) bags and on search of the bags, incriminating articles were recovered.CRIMINAL APPEAL (DB) NO. 763 OF 2026 1

It has further been alleged that on 21-02-2022, one of the search team found a suspicious person at village Harkatta Toli and on being asked, he had revealed his name as Sanjay Nagesia (A-9) and from his personal search, Naxal literature, diaries, IED materials, IEDs were seized. It has also been alleged that the Police team had subsequently apprehended three other persons and from one of the persons, namely, Markush Nagesia (A-5) a backpack was recovered containing a combat dress, 15 rounds of 5.56mm live ammunition and Naxal literature.

One Balram Oraon along with his three cadre members were also apprehended during the raid and on a search conducted, Rs. 2,09,050/- cash was recovered from him and on his disclosure, several other extremists were apprehended.CRIMINAL APPEAL (DB) NO. 763 OF 2026 2

Based on the aforesaid allegations, Peshrar P.S. Case No. 05/2022 was instituted.

4. The Central Government having regard to the gravity of the offence which involved a scheduled offence, decided that it is a fit case to be investigated by the National Investigation Agency in accordance with the National Investigation Act, 2008 and in exercise of the powers conferred under Sub-Section (5) of Section 6 read with Section 8 of the National Investigation Agency Act, 2008, the Central Government directed the National Investigation Agency to take up the investigation vide order F. No. 11011/45/2022/NIA dated 10-06-2022.

Accordingly, in compliance of the above mentioned order, NIA re-registered the FIR as RC-02/2022/NIA/RNC under Sections 147, 148, 149, 307, 353, 414 IPC, Sections 25(1A), 25(1-B)a, 26, 27, 35 of Arms Act, Sections 3, 4 and 5 of Explosive Substances Act, 1908, Section 17 of the Criminal Law Amendment Act and Section 10, 13, 14, 15 and 18 of the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967 against 17 named accused persons and 30-35 unknown active armed cadres of the terrorist organization.

5. It has been submitted by Mr. Birendra Kumar, learned counsel for the appellant that the allegations against the appellant are vague and unsubstantiated. The appellant does not have any connection with any extremist organization. It has been submitted that the appellant is in custody since 22-02-2022 and out of 186 charge sheet witnesses, only 04 witnesses have been examined so far. There is no chance of the trial being concluded in the near future. In view of the continued detention of the appellant and the conclusion of the trial facing an abyss, reference has been made to the case of Syed Iftikhar Andrabi v. National Investigation Agency, Jammu reported in 2026 INSC 503.CRIMINAL APPEAL (DB) NO. 763 OF 2026 3

Several of the protected witnesses have identified the appellant through photographs. He has further submitted that the prayer for bail of some of the co-accused persons have been rejected in Criminal Appeal (DB) No. 2144 of 2023 and Criminal Appeal (DB) No. 865 of 2025.

9. Protected witness A has named the appellant along with several others of being instrumental in planting IED explosives in the 3 km stretch of road within Bulbul jungle and on account of the IED blast, two police personnel suffered injuries. Protected witness B has also named the appellant of being involved in subversive activities and on account of the IED blasts, the legs of some police personnel were blown away. The protected witness E and F have also named the appellant. Apart from the role depicted the protected witnesses B, C, D, E, F and G have identified the appellant through photographs. 10. Mr. Birendra Kumar, learned counsel for the appellant has referred to the case of Syed Iftikhar Andrabi v. National Investigation Agency, Jammu (Supra), more particularly to the following:

"42.3. Thus, from the aforesaid figures, it is evident that the country-wide percentage of conviction under the UAP Act for the five years comprising the period 2019-23 hovers between 2% to 6%. In other words, there is 94% to 98% possibility of acquittal in such cases in the country. When it comes to the Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir, the percentage of conviction is abysmal, to say the least. For the aforesaid period, the annual rate of conviction is always less than 1%. It means that at the end of the trial, there is 99% possibility of acquittal in such cases. With these kinds of statistics staring at our face, the question is, should we continue the detention of the appellant or defer the consideration to a later stage, simply because the charges are serious?

11. The reference of the aforesaid judgment has been made in the backdrop of the period of custody undergone by the appellant and the absence of any chance of the trial being concluded in the near future as 186 witnesses have to be examined by the prosecution.

Mr. Das, learned senior counsel, however, has stated that the number of witnesses to be examined will be suitably pruned down. Even otherwise, there are overwhelming materials on record indicating the all-pervasive role played by the appellant in subversive and nefarious activities and his involvement has led to loss of life and injuries to police personnel.CRIMINAL APPEAL (DB) NO. 763 OF 2026 7

12. Since the materials collected by the investigating agency makes out a prima facie case under section 43(d)(5) of the UA(P) Act, we are not inclined to interfere in the impugned order dated 30-05-2026 passed by Sri Abhimanyu Kumar, learned Additional Judicial Commissioner-XVI-cum-Exclusive Special Judge, NIA, Ranchi, in connection with Misc.

Criminal Application No. 1027/2026 corresponding to Special (NIA) Case No. 03/2022 arising out of RC-02/2022/NIA/RNC and consequently, we dismiss this appeal.

13. Pending I.A.(s), if any, stands closed.
