10. Heard the arguments advanced by the parties and perused the case diary.

11. At the outset, it is well settled that while exercising inherent jurisdiction for quashing of an FIR or criminal proceedings, this Court is not required to undertake a meticulous appreciation of the evidence or examine whether the material collected by the prosecution would ultimately result in conviction.

The limited enquiry is whether, on the basis of the allegations and material collected during investigation, a prima facie case is disclosed and whether continuation of the proceedings would amount to abuse of the process of law.

In the present case, the investigation has already culminated in filing of the charge-sheet. The prosecution has not relied merely upon the fact that the petitioner was associated with the co-accused at the place of recovery. The case of the prosecution is that the petitioner was connected with the alleged betting network and his name surfaced during the course of investigation through the material collected by the Investigating Officer. Whether such material is admissible, reliable and sufficient to establish the petitioner's involvement, and whether the same is corroborated by other circumstances, The contention of the petitioner that his implication rests exclusively upon the memorandum statements of co-accused persons cannot, at this stage, be accepted as a ground for quashing the proceedings. The evidentiary value of such statements, the circumstances in which they were recorded, and their effect when considered along with the other material collected during investigation are matters for the Trial Court. This Court, while exercising jurisdiction under Section 528 of the BNSS, cannot undertake a detailed evaluation of the prosecution evidence as if conducting a trial. Likewise, the discrepancies pointed out by the petitioner with regard to the timing of FIR Nos.270/2022 and 271/2022, the contents of the FIRs, the alleged repetition of the independent witness, absence of a Roznamcha entry, and other alleged irregularities in the investigation, even if taken into consideration, do not, at this stage, demolish the entire prosecution case. These are essentially matters relating to the manner of investigation and appreciation of evidence, which can be appropriately examined by the learned Trial Court on the basis of the evidence adduced by the prosecution and the defence.NEUTRAL CITATION NO. 2026:MPHC-JBP:6115110 MCRC-11696-2026are matters which necessarily require appreciation of evidence during trial.
