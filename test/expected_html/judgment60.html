Date : 10.08.2026 Heard Mr. S. B. Ashad, learned counsel for the petitioner. Also heard Mr. K.K. Parashar, learned Additional Public Prosecutor appearing for the State of Assam.

2. This is an application under Section 482 of the BNSS, 2023 whereby the petitioner, namely, Rahul Basumatary is seeking Anticipatory Bail apprehending arrest in connection with an FIR, which is registered as FIR/Case No.303/2026 dated 04.07.2026 under Section 66(D) of the Information Technology Act, 2000, read with Sections 318(4) and 319(2) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, registered at CEN (Cyber Crime) Police Station, South Division, Bengaluru City, Karnataka.

3. This is an application for transit anticipatory bail filed by the petitioner, who has received a notice under Section 35 (3), BNSS for appearance before the Investigating Officer.

4. It is stated that the applicant received a Notice dated 17.07.2026 issued under Section 35(3) of the BNSS, 2023 by the Assistant Commissioner of Police, Cyber Crime Police Station, South Division, Bengaluru City, informing the applicant that during investigation of FIR/Case No. 303/2026 dated 04.07.2026 under Section 66(D) of the Information Technology Act, 2000 read with Sections 318(4) and 319(2) of the BNS, 2023 registered at CEN Crime Police Station, South Division, Bengaluru City, there are reasonable grounds to question him, and directing his appearance before the SHO within 27.07.2026, failing which the notice warns that he would be liable to arrest under Section 35 (6) of BNSS.Page No.# 3/7

The copy of the FIR has not been furnished to the applicant.

5. That, the factual matrix of the case is that the applicant, a villager of Udalguri battling carcinoma of the colon and its sequelae, was deceitfully induced by one Arif, a resident of Guwahati, Assam (mobile number 8822967399), who was known to the applicant as a friend, into parting with access to and operational control of his savings bank account with Central Bank of India, A/c No. 5381596147 (IFSC: CBIN0284540), upon false pretexts. The said Arif further cloned / fraudulently procured a duplicate of the applicant's SIM card bearing No. 8474839261- the very number registered with the bank—

thereby intercepting the OTPs and transaction alerts, and operated the said account without the knowledge, consent or benefit of the applicant. The applicant is therefore himself a victim of the said fraud and not its perpetrator.

6. That, upon receipt of the said Notice the applicant examined his statement of account and discovered that on 09/06/2026, 10/06/2026 and 15/06/2026, credits aggregating approximately Rs. 18,56,510/- (Rupees eighteen lakh fifty six thousand five hundred ten only) were received by RTGS/IMPS from persons entirely unknown to the applicant and were transferred out within hours to entities equally unknown to him, leaving only a nominal balance a pattern which itself demonstrates remote third-party operation of the account. The applicant retained no monies and derived no benefit whatsoever.

7. The applicant has, through his counsel, submitted a detailed representation dated 29/07/2026 to the Investigating Officer explaining his inability to appear by 27/07/2026, asserting his innocence, undertaking full cooperation, and seeking examination through video-conferencing or a reasonable extension of time; the applicant has further lodged / is lodging a criminal Complaint against the said Arif before the Officer-in-Charge, Udalquri Police Station and on the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal, conduct wholly inconsistent with that of a guilty person and demonstrative of his bona fides.Page No.# 4/7

8. The applicant is a patient of carcinoma of the colon (sigmoid adenocarcinoma) with ulcerative colitis, for which he underwent total proctocolectomy with ileal pouch-anal anastomosis in May 2022 followed by seven cycles of adjuvant chemotherapy; he lives with a diverting loop ileostomy (external stoma bag); he is a chronic Hepatitis-B patient on continuing antiviral therapy; he suffers from homozygous HbE haemoglobinopathy with hepatosplenomegaly; and he carries an endobiliary (CBD) stent in situ requiring periodic exchange. In such condition, sudden long-distance travel of nearly 3,000 kilometres to Bengaluru, or incarceration, would gravely imperil his life.

9. The captioned FIR is registered at Bengaluru City, Karnataka, nearly 3,000 kilometres away from the applicant's place of ordinary residence within the jurisdiction of this Hon'ble Court. The date fixed in the notice having expired on 27/07/2026, the applicant entertains an imminent and reasonable apprehension of arrest under Section 35(6) BNSS at the hands of the Karnataka police. The applicant, being gravely ill and of modest means, requires reasonable time to arrange travel with medical support and to approach the jurisdictional Court in the State of Karnataka for regular pre-arrest protection.

10. Learned counsel for the petitioner has relied upon the decision of the Hon'ble Apex Court in Priya Indoria vs. State of Karnataka & Ors., (2024) 4 SCC 749, wherein it has been observed as follows:-

"In view of what we have discussed above, we are of the view that we have discussed above, we are of the view that considering the constitutional imperative of protecting a citizen's right to life, personal liberty and dignity, the High Court or the Court of Session could grant limited anticipatory bail in the form of an interim protection under Section 438 of Cr.P.C in the interest of justice with respect to an FIR registered outside the territorial jurisdiction of the said Court, and subject to the following conditions:Page No.# 5/7
(i) Prior to passing an order of limited anticipatory bail, the investigating officer and public prosecutor who are seized of the FIR shall be issued notice on the first date of the hearing, though the Court in an appropriate case would have the discretion to grant Court in an appropriate case would have the discretion to grant interim anticipatory bail. (ii) The order of grant of limited anticipatory bail must record reasons as to why the applicant apprehends an inter-state arrest and the impact of such grant of limited anticipatory bail or interim protection, as the case may be, on the status of the investigation.
(iii) The jurisdiction in which the cognizance of the offence has been taken does not exclude the said offence from the scope of anticipatory bail by way of a State Amendment to Section 438 of Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973. (In view of the above) The applicant for anticipatory bail must satisfy the Court regarding his inability to seek anticipatory bail must satisfy the Court regarding his inablility to seek anticipatory bail from the Court which has the territorial jurisdiction to take cognizance of the offence. The grounds raised by the applicant may be – a) a reasonable and immediate threat to life, personal liberty and bodily harm in the jurisdiction where the FIR is registered, b) the apprehension of violation of right to liberty or impediments owing to arbitrariness; c) the medical status/disability of the person seeking extraterritorial limited anticipatory bail."Page No.# 6/7

11. It was also held in the said decision that if a person commits an offence in one State and the FIR is lodged within the jurisdiction where the offence was committed but the accused resides in another State he can approach the Court in the other State and seek transit anticipatory bail of limited duration.

12. What transpires from the above is that the High Court or Court of Sessions situated outside the territorial jurisdiction of the place of occurrence can grant transit anticipatory bail of a limited duration, subject to the fulfilment of the conditions stated in the aforesaid decision in Priya Indoria (supra).

13. Upon perusal of the statements made in the instant bail application supported by an affidavit, I am of the view that the conditions as laid down in Priya Indoria (supra) above has been satisfied.

14. Accordingly, I deem it fit to grant to the transit anticipatory bail to the petitioner for a limited duration of 21 days.

15. It is accordingly directed that the petitioner in the event of his arrest in connection with FIR/Case No.303/2026 dated 04.07.2026 under Section 66(D) of the Information Technology Act, 2000, read with Sections 318(4) and 319(2) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, registered at CEN (Cyber Crime) Police Station, South Division, Bengaluru City, Karnataka shall be released on interim bail of Rs. 50,000/- (Rupees fifty thousand) only with a surety of like amount to the satisfaction of the Arresting Authority.Page No.# 7/7

16. It is made clear that this order of interim protection shall be valid for the next 21 days only starting from today.

17. This Anticipatory Bail petition stands disposed of accordingly.
