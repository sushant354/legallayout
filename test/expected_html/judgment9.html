Mr. Sachin Panwar, learned counsel for the applicant. 2. Ms. Meenakshi Sharma, learned A.G.A. for the State. 3. The instant petition is preferred by the applicant Deveshwar Prasad Kala and Smt. Sunita Kala who are husband and wife for challenging the entire proceeding of Criminal Case No. 553 of 2025, arising out of Case Crime No. 46 of 2024, State vs. Deveshwar Prasad Kala and others, pending in the court of Judicial Magistrate, Rishikesh District Dehradun wherein both the applicants have been summoned to face the trial for the offences punishable under Section 420 read with Section 120B IPC. 4. It is argued by the learned counsel for the applicants that the applicants entered with an agreement with the informant on 30.03.2022 whereby the applicants agreed to sell a piece of land measuring 4000 square feet to the informant with the consideration of Rs. 1 crore out of which Rs. 50 lakhs were paid in advance through RTGS and in this agreement there is a specific disclosure that the land in question for which agreement is executed already there is a loan of Rs. 50 lakhs from Punjab National Bank. He submits that the fact that there is loan on the land
in question, the informant who is one of the signatory, being a purchaser in agreement to sell dated 30.03.2022, suppressed this fact and lodged the FIR on 06.03.2023 bearing FIR No. 0046 of 2024 at P.S. Raiwala District Dehradun for the offences punishable under Sections 420, 504 and 506 IPC wherein now the charge sheet has been filed and the applicants have been summoned. 5. The applicant is challenging the proceedings on the following grounds:- (i) The allegation as alleged in the FIR appears to be civil in nature and if the informant has any grievance he can file a regular suit. (ii) In the agreement there is a disclosure about the fact that there is a loan on the land in question but this fact he has suppressed in the FIR. (iii) The FIR has been registered without following the guidelines as formulated by the Hon'ble Apex Court in the case of Lalita Kumari vs. State of U.P. (2014) 2 SCC 1. Finally he concluded his argument by submitting that the proceeding, as initiated, is completely an abuse of process of law since informant has himself suppressed in the FIR that there is disclosure in the agreement about the loan on land in question and furthermore the FIR has been registered without holding preliminary inquiry in the light of the decision of the Hon'ble Apex Court in the case of Lalita Kumari (supra). 5. I found force in the submissions of the learned counsel for the applicant and is of the view that firstly the allegation as alleged appears to be civil arising out of an agreement dated 30.03.2022 though unregistered and secondly without following the guidelines as formulated by the Hon'ble Apex Court in the case of Lalita Kumar (supra) the FIR has been registered. 6. Issue notice to respondent no. 2. 7. Steps to be taken within a week. 8.
